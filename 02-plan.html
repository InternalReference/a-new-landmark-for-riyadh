01 · The Roadmap

A pre-concept, then delivery.

A short pre-concept, with no site work, an engineering overlay for credibility and the key interior views, is enough to raise a plot and investors. The full design, from schematic onward, follows funding, re-based on the real plot, with us as design guardian. Select a phase for its scope.

    02 · The Business Case

    Cost, funding and feasibility.

    Financial structuring is not our discipline. What follows is the indicative shape of the business case, not a valuation. The full Business and Investment Plan is the first commission of Phase 1, authored by a specialist feasibility advisor and coordinated by the Lead Design Consultant.

    Where the capital comes from

    • Equity. The client's own capital, the base of the stack.
    • Development debt. Construction finance, sized against confirmed pre-sales and leases.
    • Off-plan pre-sales. Around 430 branded residences, sold during the build, inject capital directly.
    • Pre-leasing and operators. Office pre-leases and a hotel-operator deal lock income before completion.

    Where it goes

    • Land. The most site-dependent variable, a purchase or a fund-entry route.
    • Hard construction. The dominant cost. The living facade carries a genuine premium, on the order of a few percent, confirmed in Phase 1.
    • Soft costs. Design, engineering, management, approvals and marketing.
    • Finance and contingency. Interest during construction, and a real contingency for a first-of-its-kind envelope.

    Two premiums sit on top of these mid-rise rates. A 300-metre tower carries a structural premium, on the order of tens of percent, and the living facade adds around 5%. Both are directional: no Saudi supertall benchmark is published, so they are confirmed in the Phase 1 cost model.

    Published unit rates for orientation, not a cost plan. A total depends on the final mix, specification, land and finance, none of which are fixed at this stage.

    The mixed-use programme also works as a financing structure. Residential sales provide capital during construction, and signed office and hotel leases improve the terms of the construction debt.

    Revenue and resilience

    • Residences are a one-time capital event, the least market-correlated of the six uses, and they fund the build.
    • Office, serviced apartments, hotel and retail produce recurring income, a blended stabilised yield benchmarked against Riyadh comparables. Office and hotel demand are correlated, so the genuine diversification is pairing them with residential sales and local-consumer retail.

    The load-bearing assumption is the pace of off-plan residential sales. Riyadh transaction volumes fell sharply through early 2026, while prices continued to rise. The slowdown is in liquidity, and values have not declined. The Phase 1 model treats absorption as the primary sensitivity and gates the go or no-go decision on a downside absorption case. No absorption figure is presented before the demand study exists.

    What Phase 1 delivers

    • Independent market and demand study, specific to the chosen plot
    • Detailed development-cost estimate, with the cost consultant
    • Ranged, sensitivity-tested financial model
    • Capital-stack and financing strategy
    • Risk-adjusted go or no-go recommendation

    03 · The Risks

    Key risks and mitigation.

    The main risks a project like this carries, with the mitigation for each set out in the plan.

    High
    Land accessThe strongest sites, on the park edge, are not sold to private buyers outright.
    A Phase 1 land strategy through joint venture or fund entry, with a privately acquirable fallback site carried in parallel. Land control is the first site-selection filter.
    High
    FinancingA tower at this scale is hard to fund, and this is where megaprojects stall.
    Off-plan residential sales fund construction and pre-leasing secures the debt. The full investment plan is the first Phase 1 commission.
    Medium-High
    Sector oversupplyOffice and hotel, together 37% of the programme, face a Riyadh supply peak in 2028 to 2030.
    Deliver after the peak clears, and size the hotel conservatively. The genuine diversification is pairing the correlated office and hotel with residential sales and local-consumer retail. Floors are reprogrammable.
    Medium-High
    Green maintenanceVertical greenery fails through poor long-term operation, as seen at Chengdu.
    A desert-credentialed horticulture partner and a 30-year maintenance model funded from day one, with access and species chosen for upkeep.
    Medium
    TimelineThe front-end, land and finance, can slip, and the living facade is complex.
    A conservative 7 to 8 year primary case, with the clock starting at financial close and land control, and a buffer before the 2034 World Cup.
    Medium
    ApprovalsEnvironmental impact assessment, aviation clearance and landmark floor-area ratio all require review.
    Early authority engagement, a local Architect of Record, and approvals run in parallel with design, not after it.
    Medium
    CapabilityThis would be the lead designer's first supertall.
    Met openly: a design-led vision paired with a top-tier engineering partner and a proven lead-consultant operating model. The engineering spine de-risks delivery.
    Low-Medium
    CompetitionA state-backed megatall has been proposed elsewhere in Riyadh.
    We compete on livability, greenery and urban integration. The height race belongs to the state-backed megatalls, and a private client has no reason to enter it.
